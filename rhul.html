    Egham, Surrey · Campus drive

    RoyalHolloway

    Scroll to drive round the Egham campus, from Founder's Building to the woodland. Every building sits where it really is on the campus map.

    An unofficial tribute by alumnus Alex Philip (MSc Information Security, 2022–2024). Not affiliated with or endorsed by Royal Holloway, University of London.

    Stop 1 · Founder's Building

    A French château in Surrey

    Designed by William Henry Crossland and inspired by the Château de Chambord in the Loire Valley, the red-brick Founder's Building was opened by Queen Victoria in 1886. It is Grade I listed.

    Thomas Holloway, a Victorian entrepreneur and philanthropist, founded the college here on the Mount Lee estate in 1879.

    Inside: Student rooms, the dining hall, chapel, picture gallery and a lecture theatre

    Quads: Queen Victoria's statue in the North Quad; Thomas and Jane Holloway in the South Quad

    Stop 2 · Chapel & Picture Gallery

    Art and evensong

    The Victorian chapel hosts Choral Evensong, Compline and Catholic Masses, and is known for daily sung morning services, which are rare at UK universities. The Chapel Choir gives around 50 concerts a year.

    Holloway bought 77 paintings for the Picture Gallery between 1881 and 1883, including works by Millais, Burne-Jones and Frith.

    Gallery: Open to visitors on Wednesdays in term time

    Stop 3 · Emily Wilding Davison Building

    The library on Founder's Square

    Opened in 2017 and named after alumna and suffragette Emily Wilding Davison, it holds the Library, the Student Services Centre, the Careers and Enterprise Hub and an exhibition space.

    Its roof terrace looks out over the campus towards London and Heathrow.

    Opened: 18 October 2017, by The Princess Royal

    Outside: Founder's Square, a paved courtyard for events

    Stop 4 · The academic quarter

    Bedford, McCrea and Moore

    The Bedford Building is home to the School of Engineering, Physical and Mathematical Sciences. The McCrea Building houses Law and Social Sciences, and the Moore Building the School of Business and Management.

    The name Bedford comes from Bedford College, founded in 1849 by Elizabeth Jesser Reid, which merged with Royal Holloway in 1985.

    Cyber: The Information Security Group launched its MSc in Information Security in 1992, reportedly the first postgraduate course of its kind

    Stop 5 · Boilerhouse & George Eliot

    Coffee in a Victorian boiler house

    The Boilerhouse Café sits in a historic Victorian building with a cobbled courtyard, and gives its coffee grounds away for composting.

    George Eliot Halls, on the northern side of campus, are self-catered townhouses with shared kitchens and lounges.

    Stop 6 · Students' Union & Katharine Worth

    Union Plaza

    The Students' Union has a large function hall, two bars and Tommy's Kitchen. Union Plaza out front has a permanent canopy, benches and food vans, with a weekly street-food market.

    The student-run Insanity Radio broadcasts from campus on 103.2FM. Nearby, the Katharine Worth Building is home to the School of Performing and Digital Arts.

    Stop 7 · Wolfson & Beatrice Shilling

    Labs with a wind turbine

    The Wolfson Building houses the School of Life Sciences and the Environment.

    The Beatrice Shilling Building, opened in 2018–19, is home to Electronic Engineering. Its rooftop field laboratory has solar panels and a wind turbine. It is named after the engineer whose fix to British fighter engines mattered early in the Second World War.

    Stop 8 · The Hub

    Food and hangouts

    The Hub serves hot dishes, burgers, vegetarian and vegan meals and the Kimiko Japanese Kitchen. Downstairs, Imagine is a quieter spot for bubble tea or ice cream.

    Stop 9 · Sports Centre & halls

    Pitches under floodlights

    The Sports Centre was refurbished in 2013 and its floodlit pitches opened for the 2014/15 season. The hockey club, the largest sports club on campus, plays on the hockey pitch, and the all-weather 3G pitch is FA-accredited for 11-a-side games.

    Gowar and Wedderburn halls, at the southern end of campus, opened in September 2004 with 570 en-suite rooms.

    Stop 10 · Woodland Walk & Meadow

    A living campus

    Much of the campus is woodland and green space managed for wildlife. The Woodland Walk passes a wooden bridge over a pond, and the Meadow, beyond Jane Holloway Hall, is part of the Bee Walk past a beehive.

    End of the tour

    Thanks for visiting.
